T1 mapping from variable flip angles

A short note on the DESPOT1 signal model

Signal model

For a spoiled gradient-echo (SPGR) sequence at steady state, with repetition time TR and flip angle α, the signal is

S(α) = M0 · sin α · (1 − E1) / (1 − E1 cos α),    E1 = exp(−TR / T1)

M0 absorbs the proton density and receive-coil scaling, so it is unknown. Acquiring at two or more flip angles gives enough equations to solve for both M0 and T1 in each voxel.

Linearized fit

Rearranging the signal equation gives a straight line:

S / sin α = E1 · S / tan α + M0 (1 − E1)

Plotting S / sin α against S / tan α over the flip angles, the slope is E1, so

T1 = −TR / ln(slope)

Things that bias the result

  • B1 inhomogeneity. The actual flip angle is k · α, where k varies across space. Using the nominal α biases T1, so a B1 map is needed for accurate values, especially at 3T and above.
  • Imperfect spoiling. The model assumes the transverse magnetization is fully spoiled between TRs.
  • Magnetization transfer. In tissue with a macromolecular pool, a single-pool model can bias the apparent T1.
  • Flip-angle choice. Precision depends on which angles are used; angles around the Ernst angle for the expected T1 are most informative.
