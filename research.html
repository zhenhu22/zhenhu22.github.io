Research

I work on quantitative MRI, with a focus on pulse sequence design and reconstruction for fast, whole-brain T1 mapping.

Current work

Rapid 3D whole-brain T1 mapping with inversion recovery

Inversion recovery is the standard reference for T1 mapping, but it is slow. I developed an inversion recovery T1 mapping technique with a long 3D stack-of-spirals turbo FLASH readout, with a presaturation pulse and short saturation time before inversion, implemented on a Philips scanner. Combined with SENSE and compressed-sensing SENSE reconstruction of undersampled data, it covers the whole brain at 1.0 mm isotropic resolution in 3–4 minutes, and was compared against a conventional 2D EPI inversion recovery sequence.

Dual flip angle T1 mapping and magnetization transfer

In in vivo dual flip angle (DFA) T1 mapping, the estimates depend on the flip angle and repetition time combination used, even after B1+ correction. In phantoms the dependence is negligible, and I proposed that magnetization transfer (MT) between water protons and macromolecular protons contributes. A two-pool cross-relaxation simulation is consistent with the in vivo measurements and suggests that optimized acquisition parameters can reduce the variation. Combining DFA with a stack-of-spirals readout and retrospective undersampling gives whole-brain 1.0 mm isotropic T1 maps in about one minute.

T1 and macromolecular proton fraction from inversion recovery

In the two-pool MT model, the slow-relaxation coefficient of the inversion recovery curve, and the inversion degree derived from it, decrease monotonically with the macromolecular proton fraction. A monoexponential three-parameter fit of the IR curve therefore yields apparent T1 and inversion degree together, and the latter gives the macromolecular proton fraction without a biexponential fit or explicit MT parameters. This is validated in simulation, phantoms, and in vivo brain.

Model-based reconstruction for T2 mapping

A projection-onto-convex-sets reconstruction (POCSENSE) for sensitivity-encoded data, extended with a T2 decay model for model-consistent reconstruction and combined with spatial compressed sensing, tested on a Shepp–Logan digital phantom.
